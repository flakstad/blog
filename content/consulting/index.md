My work is hands-on from product definition through implementation. I help clarify uncertain problems, make architecture and technology choices, and build the software through to production.

## Situations where I’m useful

- Building an important new product or technically difficult product capability.
- Working out the product and technical direction when requirements and technology choices are still unclear.
- Taking a prototype or early product into reliable production.
- Getting a stalled product or subsystem moving again.
- De-risking a rewrite, migration or major technical transition.
- Improving the reliability and testing of a critical system.

I work broadly as a product engineer, not as a generic AI consultant. That said, I have deep recent experience building LLM and agent systems for production: evidence-backed research and analysis, citations and provenance, retrieval, AI chat, asynchronous execution, tool use, sandboxed code execution, agent workflows, and agent-facing CLIs and developer tooling. This has included the full product surface around the models, from interaction and streaming UX to backend execution and production reliability.

Voice AI is another specialist area. A convincing demo can be assembled quickly; making one reliable across real callers and consequential workflows is a dramatically harder engineering problem. Through roughly a year building Kari, I’ve worked with callers who vary in language, dialect, audio quality, speaking style and intent, and who interrupt, overlap, go silent or say things the system was never designed around. Tool failures and ambiguous requests add more state to conversations that already have an effectively unbounded input space.

Prompt iteration is essential but nowhere near sufficient. Reliable operation has required explicit conversational modes, purpose-built tools, deterministic logic around operations the model should not improvise, deliberate formatting of values such as dates before they are spoken, sidecar handling for sensitive structured input such as Norwegian national identity numbers, programmatic audio checks, stall detection and nudging when the model fails to speak, and robust tool-state handling. Much of this has been learned through actual callers using appointment booking, change and cancellation workflows, including healthcare-related cases where operational correctness matters. I’m willing to consult on serious production voice-AI systems where this experience is useful, but it is one specialization within broader product engineering.

## A bounded first step

When the problem is uncertain, a **Technical Product Assessment** can be a useful first engagement. Over 5–8 working days, I inspect the actual product, code and available evidence, investigate the important technical and product risks, and build focused spikes, tests or prototypes where they provide better answers.

The result is a set of concrete findings and recommendations, with a realistic next implementation milestone. It is not a strategy workshop or a generic report. For Norwegian clients, a typical assessment is in the range of NOK 80,000–125,000 excluding VAT, depending on scope.

Not every engagement needs an assessment. Follow-on work can use fixed-price milestones, reserved weekly capacity, or time-based billing where uncertainty remains.
